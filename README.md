# moonline

Five crossings, and the voyage beyond them. One intensement — a re-feel of the forces at play in the universe:
impact, light, heat, current, gravity. The science is the antagonist.

MOONLINE emerges through **The Last Platform**, the fifth world of
[Codified Resonance](https://codifiedresonance.com).

**Enter here → [codifiedresonance.com/last-platform/moonline](https://codifiedresonance.com/last-platform/moonline)**

## the worlds

| | world | place | force |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | The Crossing | Earth station → the Moon | impact · the long emptiness |
| 02 | Ringfall | Saturn's rings | information · can you see in time |
| 03 | Ironlight | KELT-9b, the hottest known orbit | heat · you cannot dodge it, only budget it |
| 04 | The Behemoth | GJ 436b, an exhaled sky | current · the medium itself moves you |
| 05 | The Well | a spinning supermassive black hole | gravity · the deep line costs you time itself |

Edition I is the proving ground. **Edition II is the voyage**, outward from home in order of
real distance: Baltis 01, Mariner 02, Plume 03, Fathom 04, Metronome 05, and then the unseen. Four have emerged:

| | world | place | force |
|---|---|---|---|
| II · 02 | Mariner | Valles Marineris, Mars | confinement · a canyon 4,000 km long and 7 deep, and the walls do not move |
| II · 03 | Crescent | Io, the most volcanic world we know | the squeeze, and the dose · Jupiter's radiation counts every second you linger |
| II · 04 | Fathom | Neptune, the planet found by mathematics | wind, then weight · the fastest winds in the solar system, then a dark that ends in diamond |
| II · 05 | Metronome | CP 1919 (PSR B1919+21), the first pulsar ever found | rhythm · a beam no eye has seen turns once every 1.3373 s, and acts on *when* |

Mariner is flown end to end, west to east: from the dawn fog of Noctis Labyrinthus, through the narrows of
Ius and the width of Melas, into a dust storm down Coprates, through the collapsed blocks of Eos Chaos and out onto
Chryse at dusk, with the sun setting behind you. The canyon is the seed's, carved fresh every week. The air is
thin, so nothing but thrust turns you; it is thickest on the floor, seven kilometres down, where TRAWLER feeds;
the sun reaches CARAVEL only where the canyon opens or high on the walls; PENDULUM banks every metre it falls.
Rock comes down the walls, mesas stand where the floor fell away, and a dust devil will shove you, and clean your
panels as it does, the way wind cleaned Spirit's and Opportunity's for years.

Crescent skims Io, low over the sulphur plains, with Jupiter filling a quarter of the sky. Its first plume was
seen in March 1979 by Linda Morabito, as a faint crescent off Io's edge in a Voyager 1 image taken to steer by;
the crossing opens on one, lit from behind. Jupiter's tides squeeze Io until it erupts: the plumes are columns of
sulphur gas that lift you, opening into umbrellas that fall back as frost, pecking at the machine. Then Loki's lava
lake, the eclipse where Io's thin air freezes and falls, Pele's great umbrella over its red ring, and the flux tube,
the current between Io and Jupiter, where the radiation peaks. The dose keeps count the whole way and leaves its
mark: speed is the only shield. TRAWLER feeds on the plumes, the only air there is; CARAVEL has a twenty-seventh of
Earth's sunlight, but a plume fills the sail; PENDULUM banks every rise and fall. The cargo is images, fogging in
the radiation.

Fathom is the first world you go down into rather than across. Neptune was found in 1846 with mathematics,
from the way it tugged at Uranus, before anyone had seen it; only Voyager 2 has been there. You fall through the
haze into the bands, jets near 2,400 km/h that change direction as you fall through them: the moving air drags the
machine toward its own motion, so let go of the controls and the sky takes you, sideways, up, out. Then the edge
of a great dark storm that turns you round its eye, the black below the clouds where the only light is the
machine's, the hot thick mantle where everything slows, and at the bottom the diamonds, falling with you. CARAVEL
has almost no sun this far out and sails on the crosswind instead; PENDULUM goes heavy and banks the gusts it
refuses; TRAWLER feeds on the hydrogen that thickens as you fall; VECTOR fights it all, and the deep is hot. The
cargo is a sealed sphere of instruments, carried down under rising weight.

Metronome's beam is radio, as the real one is: it is never drawn and the sky never flashes. You hear
each pulse in the receiver and read it on a chart recorder at the bottom of the screen — the way Jocelyn
Bell Burnell first found it in 1967, as a bit of scruff on the paper at 81.5 MHz — with a faint mark
ahead of the pen where the next pulse will land.

In Edition II the machine remembers. A knock leaves a **scar** you can see and hear, with a small,
honest effect on how it flies. A hangar crew mends it in real time while you are away: half in twelve
hours, whole in a day, and flying sooner is a request for early release. Each crossing carries
**cargo** that must arrive whole (on Mars, solar panels kept clear of dust; over Io, images kept clear of the radiation; in Neptune, a sealed sphere carried down; in Metronome, a clock kept in phase with the beat): exposure wears
it down over time, impact all at once. A line carries the machine's condition at the start, so a
scarred machine re-runs scarred.

**hand the machine on** gives someone your machine, not just your line: every hand's line, and the
condition each began with. Whoever opens it re-runs the whole chain from nothing, hand by hand, then
flies the machine as the last hand left it. No crew works on a machine between hands.

## the universe

The game works out the current week for itself and builds every world from it —
no server, nothing published, nothing to maintain. Everyone who opens MOONLINE
in a given week is crossing the identical universe, automatically, and every seven
Earth days it becomes a new one. The grid names it: `universe 31786 · this week's`.

A specific universe can be visited directly with `?seed=` — for example
`/crossing/?seed=7F3A`.

## playing

Arrows or WASD steer; space holds (each machine's hold is its own); R restarts; M mutes.
A controller works too: left stick steers, A or a trigger holds, Start restarts. On a phone,
drag anywhere to steer and hold the button bottom right.

## the line

Cross a world and you may leave your line: a recording of exactly how you
crossed — the seed, and your hands tick by tick. It is held on your own device,
yours to keep or to hand to another witness. Nothing is sent anywhere. There is
no account, no ladder, no season.

Because a crossing is built from its seed and computed at a fixed rate, the same
seed and the same inputs reproduce the same crossing exactly, on any machine.
A line is not believed. It is re-run, and witnessed.

So that a line re-runs the same in every browser, the simulation does its own sine, cosine and
arctangent (ported from fdlibm, using only arithmetic IEEE-754 defines exactly) rather than the
browser's, which the JavaScript standard allows to differ in the last digits.

Your best crossing of each universe flies with you the next time, as a faint
machine and a thread of light. To hand a line on, use **hand on your line** on
the arrival card: it makes a link carrying the universe, the machine and your
hands. Whoever opens it watches their browser re-run the line from nothing and
show the time it actually gets — then the line flies beside them. If a world's
physics has changed since, the line says it was crossed in an older version of
that world instead of pretending.

**let it travel** films your crossing for sharing: the page re-runs your line and films it
vertically — the fastest stretch, the closest pass, the line and the hand-off — then the universe
speaks, then the world's name and the invitation. Not a screen capture: a line, re-run.

## sound

Sound is placed where it physically is. A vacuum carries nothing, so a machine is heard only through its
own body: a low pressure through the frame, rising under load, never a tone in the air. VECTOR is a
combustion rumble with a pump far inside it; PENDULUM's escapement ticks through the frame; CARAVEL has no
engine, and you hear only its rigging creak as the light changes; TRAWLER is the hum of its intake. The
velocity, the wind and the tearing hiss through the stars at warp, is the speed itself, and stays.
A crossing does not count down. Three clamps let go, felt through the hull, and you are free. In Metronome
you are released on the pulsar's third pulse. On arrival the drive spools down through the frame.
Each frame also carries a hull band, the structure resonating in the low mids where the ear lives, so the
machine still comes through on earphones and small speakers; VECTOR's and TRAWLER's rumble breathes with a
combustion flutter.

## station control

*Built, and resting.* Station control is switched off for now (`COMMS_LIVE` in each world): the crossings
are not yet long enough to call for it. Everything below is in place for when they are.

A human voice on the radio, and the words follow the light. From the Moon home answers live, a few seconds
late, because your news takes 1.3 seconds to reach them and their answer 1.3 seconds to come back; at lunar
approach home hands you over to Tranquility. At Mars home is as many light-minutes away as Mars really is today,
worked out from the date, so what it says is already old: a warning about the narrows arrives once you are through
them. At Saturn home's messages are an hour and a quarter old when they arrive. Everywhere further out nothing sent could reach you in time, so home recorded everything before you
left, including the messages for if something goes wrong ("If you're hearing this…"). In the Well, if you are
lost, the signal just stops.

Edition II is one voyage and the radio carries through it: home knows where else you have been, and Mars is the
last place on the way out where you can still hear it answer.

It comes to know you. It remembers your first crossing of each world, how many times you have been back, how
long you have been away, whether you were lost last time, and whether you have just beaten your best. It
prefers what it hasn't said to you before, and says less on ground you know. She speaks with your browser's own
voice: a British woman's, a natural one where the device has it, with the transcript on screen. **comms** at the top, or
C, switches it off; with the sound off the transcript still comes through. Its memory stays on your device.
It never speaks while a line is being witnessed or filmed, and it never touches the simulation.

## how it's built

Each world is one HTML file. No build step, no bundler, no framework — open any
of them and read the source. Three.js is the only dependency, loaded from a CDN.
Sound is synthesised in the browser; there are no audio files. All textures are
drawn in code at load.

Deployed with GitHub Pages. Each world is served from its own folder, so the URL
is the world's name.

## the descent

The page on codifiedresonance.com that leads into the worlds is served from here too: `descent/descent.css`
and `descent/descent.js`. The embed on the site is a few lines that load them, so the descent changes
when this repo is pushed, with nothing to paste.

A crossing reaches the descent two ways. The way home carries it (`?crossed=`), and the descent also loads
`descent/bridge.html` unseen from the worlds' own origin: it reads which worlds have a line on this device and
tells the page, so a crossing counts however the witness came home (the back button, leave, a direct link). Only
the names of the worlds pass between them, inside the browser. "Forget my crossings" is respected: the bridge
does not bring back what was forgotten.

The Last Platform's node page (`codifiedresonance.com/last-platform/node`) is served the same way, from
`platform/node.css` and `platform/node.js`. To add a game to the platform, add one object to the
`EMERGENCES` array in `platform/node.js` and push.
